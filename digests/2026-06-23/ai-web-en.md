This economic research paper analyzes ~400,000 anonymized Claude Code sessions from October 2025 to April 2026. Key findings include: a clear division of labor—humans make most planning decisions while Claude handles execution; domain expertise amplifies Claude’s per-instruction output; every major occupation succeeds at coding tasks at rates close to software engineers; and the gap between intermediate and expert users in success rate is modest. The research offers a rare empirical window into how agentic coding is evolving in the wild, with implications for workforce productivity, skill demand, and AI-assisted task complexity.
